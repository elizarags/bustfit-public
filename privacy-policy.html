Privacy Policy

Last updated: 28 October 2026  ·  Applies to the Bustfit Chrome extension and the Bustfit email list

The short version: Bustfit stores your size and preferences on your device, with a few disclosed exceptions: problem reports you choose to send include it (Section 6), and feedback you send from the panel includes your sizing only if you tick the box (Section 16). Bustfit's own backend handles a few other features (image analysis and review summaries, styling tips, pooled anonymous sizing feedback, optional feedback from the panel, and problem reports you choose to send us) — none of it is tied to your identity, and we don't sell anything about you to anyone.

When you use features that require talking to the internet — like image analysis, styling tips, fetching reviews, sharing anonymous sizing feedback, answering a feedback question, or reporting a problem — we're clear below about exactly what's sent, where it goes, and why.

1. What Bustfit is and how it works

Bustfit is a Chrome extension that analyses clothing products and gives you a fit score based on your bust measurements and preferences. All scoring logic runs locally inside the extension.

A small number of features use Bustfit's own backend server (image analysis, review summaries, styling tips, pooled anonymous sizing feedback, and problem reports you choose to send). None of these send anything that identifies you personally by name, email, or account — problem reports are the one exception where your band/cup size is included, for the reasons explained in Section 6. Each is covered in detail below.

2. Data stored on your device

Everything in this section lives in Chrome's local extension storage on your device. It is not synced to Chrome's cloud sync and not accessible to any website or third party. It is not transmitted to Bustfit except where a later section says so (Sections 4, 6, 7 and 16).

What's storedWhat it contains
Your preferences Band size, cup size, bra style preference, strapless bra preference, typical fit challenges, usual clothing size and region, auto-open panel setting
Scored products A short-term cache of recent fit scores so the panel loads instantly when you reopen it. Cleared when you navigate away.
Review summary cache AI-generated summaries of bust-relevant reviews for products you've scored, kept for 7 days so we don't re-query the API unnecessarily.
Order history Order number, product name(s), purchase date, and estimated delivery date for purchases detected on supported retailers. Used only to time a feedback prompt. See Section 5.
Recent products The last 50 products you scored (product ID, title, timestamp). Used to match order confirmation pages against products you already have scores for.
Usage analytics Counts of: products scored, retailer links clicked, purchases detected, feedback submitted. Stored locally only. See Section 9.
Admin product notes Notes added via the admin editor, stored per product URL. Only relevant if you use the admin features.
Anonymous device ID A random identifier generated on your device (not tied to your name, email, or any account) and stored locally. Used to attribute API requests and pooled sizing feedback to "one device" — e.g. so the same device can't submit sizing feedback for the same product twice — and to count how many devices use Bustfit, and on how many different days, so we can see whether it is useful. That count covers devices, not people, and is never linked to your name, email or size. See Sections 4 and 7.
Your sizing feedback Your own post-purchase answers (size ordered, how it fit) for products you've bought. A copy is submitted anonymously to Bustfit's backend — see Section 7.
Apart from the specific features described in Sections 4, 6, 7 and 16, none of this data leaves your device. It stays there until you clear the extension's storage or uninstall the extension.

3. Data read from retailer pages

When you visit a product page on a supported retailer (ASOS, Nordstrom, Revolve, and others), the extension reads the product title, description, fabric information, neckline details, and the main product image URL from the page. On some retailers (for example Revolve and ASOS) it also reads the photographed model's published measurements or height and size worn, used only to caption the estimated bust coverage. This information is used locally to calculate a fit score. It is not stored beyond the current session unless you've previously scored the same product (in which case the score is cached locally — see above).

The extension does not read your browsing history, your account details, your saved payment methods, your basket contents, or any other information on retailer pages beyond the specific product fields needed for scoring.

4. Anthropic API — image analysis, review summaries, and styling tips

Bustfit uses the Anthropic API for three features: garment image analysis, review summarisation, and styling tips. API requests are made on your behalf using Bustfit's own API credentials — you do not need to provide or manage an API key.

Image analysis: When scoring a product, the extension sends the product image URL (and, for some products, the URLs of a few more of its photos) to Bustfit's backend, which asks Anthropic's API to identify garment construction details (neckline type, strap style, silhouette, how much of the bust the garment covers). For a few retailers whose image servers block this, the extension downloads the product image itself and sends the image to our backend instead of its URL. Results are cached on our servers by image URL so the same product isn't analysed twice. No personal information — not your size, name, or account details — is included.

Review summaries: When bust-relevant reviews are found for a product, excerpts of those reviews (plain text only, no reviewer names or identifiers), along with the product's ID and name, are sent through Bustfit's backend to Anthropic's API to generate a brief summary. Again, no personal information is included.

Styling tips: When generating a styling note for a product, the extension sends the product's name, its detected construction, fabric and neckline details, the brand note shown in the panel (if any), and the panel's own bra reading for the product (for example "built-in shelf bra only — unlikely to provide enough support at your size") to Bustfit's backend, which passes them to Anthropic's API. Your size and preferences are not sent. The bra reading is worked out from your size and bra preferences, but it doesn't contain them. No name, email, or account details are ever included.

Each of these requests carries the anonymous device ID described in Section 2, and our backend keeps your IP address only as a keyed one-way hash, to apply usage limits (see Section 13).

These requests are subject to Anthropic's Privacy Policy. We recommend reviewing Anthropic's policy to understand how they handle API request data. Product images or their URLs, bust-relevant review text, product names, construction details and the panel's bra reading are sent to Anthropic's servers for processing. Your size is not.

5. Purchase detection

On supported retailer websites, the extension detects when you reach an order confirmation page. When it does, it reads three things from the page:

  • Your order number
  • The name(s) of the item(s) you ordered
  • Your estimated delivery date (if shown on the page)

This information is stored locally on your device and used for one purpose only: to prompt you for post-purchase fit feedback after you've received your order. It is never transmitted externally.

The extension does not read your payment details, delivery address, or any other information from order confirmation pages.

Supported retailers include Shopify-based stores, ASOS, Nordstrom, Net-a-Porter, Mytheresa, and WooCommerce stores. You can see the full list in the extension source code.

6. Reporting a problem & automatic error monitoring

If you use the "report a problem" button in the panel to flag something wrong with a score or the extension, the extension sends the following to Bustfit's backend, where it's stored in our database so we can investigate and reproduce the issue:

  • The URL of the product page you were on
  • What you were reporting (e.g. "wrong score") and which panel tab you were on
  • Your band size and cup size, and the broad body-shape category worked out from them
  • Any free-text note you choose to add describing the problem
  • Your browser's user agent string and a timestamp

This is entirely optional and only happens when you actively click "report a problem" and submit — nothing is sent unless you do this. We don't ask for your name or email as part of this flow, but if you include either in your free-text note, that text is stored in our database as you wrote it.

A copy of each report — the page URL, what you reported and which tab you were on, your note, and the timestamp, but not your size — is also emailed to us through Resend, an email delivery service.

This report is also sent to Sentry, the third-party error-tracking service we use to catch and fix bugs. Sentry does not receive your band/cup size, the body-shape category worked out from it, or your free-text note — those are stripped out before the report reaches Sentry — but it does receive the page URL, report type, panel tab, your browser's user agent, and the timestamp.

Separately, Bustfit also uses Sentry to automatically catch unexpected errors (for example, if a scoring request fails). This automatic monitoring sends only the error, which part of the extension it came from, and the retailer's website — never your size, the body-shape category worked out from it, your preferences, or anything you typed. Errors on Bustfit's own servers are reported to Sentry in the same way, without the contents of your request.

7. Sizing feedback (shared, anonymous)

After a detected purchase (Section 5), or any time you use the sizing feedback prompt in the Sizing tab, you can optionally log what size you ordered and how it fit. This is always stored locally on your device first.

A copy of that same answer — the size you ordered, how it fit, and the retailer's domain — is also submitted to Bustfit's backend, tagged with the anonymous device ID described in Section 2. Nothing that could identify you is included: no name, email, account, order number, exact measurements, or free-text notes you may have added locally. In particular, sizing feedback never includes your band or cup size — only the size-ordered category and the fit outcome. (Feedback you send from the panel, Section 16, is separate and can include your sizing, but only if you tick the box.)

Bustfit pools this feedback across everyone who submits it for the same product, and shows aggregate counts (e.g. "3 people sized up, fit well") to other shoppers looking at that same product — never individual submissions. Aggregates only appear once at least 3 people have submitted feedback for a product, so a single person's answer is never shown on its own.

Each device can submit feedback once per product. If you don't want to participate, simply don't use the sizing feedback prompt — nothing is submitted unless you actively answer it.

8. External services used to fetch reviews

Bustfit reads reviews from the retailer's own review platform. On many supported retailers, reviews are hosted by a review service such as Yotpo or Bazaarvoice, or by the retailer itself. The extension requests that product's reviews from the service directly, using only the product's ID.

These requests don't include your size, preferences, or any identifying information. As with any web request, the review service sees your IP address.

Earlier versions also searched Reddit and the wider web (through DuckDuckGo) for reviews. Bustfit no longer does either.

9. Usage analytics

Bustfit tracks four anonymous usage counts, stored locally on your device:

  • Number of products scored
  • Number of retailer links clicked from the panel
  • Number of purchases detected
  • Number of fit feedback submissions completed

These counts are stored as part of an event log (capped at 500 entries) in local extension storage. They are never transmitted automatically. If you use the admin export feature, you can export this data manually — that export goes only to wherever you choose to save it.

There is no analytics service, no tracking pixel, and no beacon of any kind in the extension that reports your behaviour back to us. (Emails from the Bustfit email list are different — see Section 17.)

10. Editor notes (hosted JSON)

Bustfit fetches a public JSON file from jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) that contains editor-curated notes about specific products and brand fit patterns. This file is publicly accessible to anyone. The request to fetch it includes no user data — it is the same as loading any public web resource.

The cached copy of this file is stored locally on your device and refreshed every 24 hours.

11. Retailer link tagging

When you click a retailer link from within the Bustfit panel, the extension appends UTM attribution tags to the URL. These tags look like: utm_source=bustfit&utm_content=good-fit.

These tags identify that the click came from Bustfit and which score tier the product received. They contain no personal information — no size, no preferences, no identifier tied to you as an individual.

This tagging exists to support future affiliate partnerships that help sustain Bustfit as a free tool. The retailer may record the click as part of their normal web analytics.

12. What we do not collect

Bustfit does not:
  • Collect your name, email address, or any contact information through the extension — unless you choose to type one into a free-text problem report (Section 6) or a feedback note (Section 16); we never ask for either. (If you joined the Bustfit email list on our website, we have the address you gave us there — see Section 17)
  • Collect financial data, payment details, or card numbers
  • Track your browsing history beyond the product page currently open
  • Store or transmit your exact bra size to a server, except in two specific, disclosed cases: problem reports (Section 6) include it, for the reasons explained there, and feedback you send from the panel (Section 16) includes it only if you tick the box. Sizing feedback (Section 7) is not one of these — it only ever shares a size-ordered category (e.g. "sized up one") and a fit outcome, never your stored band/cup measurements
  • Tie anything sent to our backend to your identity — requests, pooled sizing feedback, and feedback from the panel use only the anonymous, on-device ID described in Section 2 (problem reports, Section 6, use no ID at all). Your IP address is kept only as a keyed one-way hash, used only for usage limits, and deleted after 30 days (Section 13)
  • Sell, rent, or share any data with advertisers or data brokers
  • Use your data for advertising targeting of any kind
  • Aggregate personal profiles of individual users — sizing feedback is pooled per-product, not per-person, and only ever shown once at least 3 people have contributed; feedback from the panel is used only to improve how Bustfit reads products and is never shown to anyone

13. Data retention and deletion

All locally stored data (preferences, scores, order history, analytics, cached notes) can be cleared at any time by removing the extension from Chrome, or by clearing the extension's storage via Chrome's developer tools.

Review summary caches expire automatically after 7 days. The recent products list is capped at 50 entries (oldest removed automatically). The analytics event log is capped at 500 entries.

Most of what Bustfit does never touches our servers at all (see Section 2). The exceptions are:

  • Sizing feedback (Section 7) isn't tied to your identity and is pooled with other submissions rather than kept per-person.
  • Feedback from the panel (Section 16) is deleted automatically 90 days after you last sent it for that product.
  • Problem reports (Section 6) are sent to us on purpose and kept so we can fix bugs.
  • IP addresses. For requests for image analysis, review summaries, styling tips, brand information, and feedback from the panel, our backend keeps the IP address only as a keyed one-way hash: the address is passed through the hash before anything is stored, and the address itself is never stored. The hash lets it count how many requests one address makes in a day (and, for image analysis, in ten minutes, to stop runaway loops) so it can apply usage limits. It can tell repeat requests apart but can't be turned back into your address. The hashes are deleted automatically after 30 days. Our hosting provider may also keep standard request logs, which include IP addresses, under its own retention policy.

If you'd like something you sent removed sooner — a problem report or your feedback from the panel — contact us (Section 18) with the approximate date and the product or what you reported, and we'll do our best to locate and remove it. None of it is indexed by anything that identifies you, so locating a specific item depends on the details you can give us.

14. Children's privacy

Bustfit is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13.

15. Changes to this policy

If we make meaningful changes to this policy, we will update the "last updated" date at the top of this page. For significant changes — particularly any that would involve sending new categories of data externally — we will also notify users via the extension itself (via the popup or panel).

Continuing to use Bustfit after a policy update means you've seen and accept the updated policy.

16. Feedback from the panel (optional)

The panel asks a few short questions so we can check how well Bustfit reads products. Each one stays closed until you tap it, and nothing is sent unless you answer and tap Send. The questions are:

  • Under the fit score: "Does this score feel accurate?" — yes, no, or unsure, and after a no, whether it should be higher or lower.
  • Under the bust coverage estimate: "Does this feel accurate?" — yes or no, and after a no, whether it should be higher or lower.
  • "Something feel off?" — a note in your own words about anything else.
  • Under a sweater's, blazer's, coat's or cardigan's shape label: "Does this match the sweater?" (or blazer, coat or cardigan) — yes or no, and after a no, which shape it's closer to: Shows your shape, Falls straight, or Adds volume. When the listing doesn't say how it's cut, the question is "Can you see how it's cut?" instead, answered Fitted, Straight or Oversized.
  • Under a blazer's, coat's or cardigan's "Closing it" line: "Have you worn it? Did it close over your bust?" — Closes, Gapes a little, Too tight to close, or I wear it open (by choice). For a wrap style it asks how it wrapped over your bust: Wraps comfortably, Gapes at the neckline, or Too small to wrap. It isn't asked on an open-front style with no buttons.

The "Did it close over your bust?" answer is different from the others: it describes how a garment fit you, on your body, and it tells us you have worn it. Like every question here it's optional, and it's sent only when you tap Send.

When you send an answer, the extension sends the following to Bustfit's backend:

  • Which question you answered, and your answer (including the follow-up choice after a no)
  • Any note you choose to type
  • The product's name, its web address (without any tracking parameters), the retailer's website, and what you were shown (the score, the coverage estimate, or the shape label or "Closing it" result)
  • Which version of the panel's Sizing and Community tabs you were using (the current design or the earlier one some testers can switch back to)
  • The anonymous device ID described in Section 2, and the time you sent it

Your sizing is separate and optional. If your profile has a size saved, every question offers a checkbox, unticked by default, that lets you include your sizing with that one answer. The exact values that would be sent are shown next to it before you decide: your band and cup size (converted to UK sizing so they can be compared) and the sizing system you use, and your usual clothing size and region. If you leave it unticked, none of your sizing is sent — and our server discards any sizing that arrives without that choice. So a "Did it close over your bust?" answer is stored with your bra size only if you tick that box.

We use this feedback only to improve how Bustfit reads products: an answer never changes a score, a shape label or a "Closing it" line by itself. It's never shown to other shoppers and isn't sent to Sentry, including when something goes wrong. Each device keeps one answer per product for each question: sending again replaces your earlier answer. Feedback is deleted automatically 90 days after you last sent it for that product (Section 13). We don't ask for your name or email; if you type either into the note, it's stored as you wrote it.

17. The Bustfit email list

This section is about the email list you can join on our website. It is separate from the extension: the extension never asks for your email address, and nothing in the extension is linked to it.

If you sign up, the email address you enter is stored by Resend, the email delivery service we use to send you a welcome email and occasional updates about Bustfit. Our own database keeps only Resend's internal reference for your signup, so that you are sent the welcome email once — not your address.

Link clicks. Links in emails from the list pass through a Bustfit tracking address before taking you to the page, so we can see which links were clicked. Resend records each click against your email address, along with the time and the technical details your email app sends when it follows a link (such as your IP address and the type of device or app). We don't track whether emails are opened. We use click information only to see whether our emails are useful and which links people follow. It isn't combined with anything from the extension, and we don't sell it or use it for advertising.

You can leave the list at any time using the unsubscribe link in any email, or by contacting us (Section 18), and we'll remove your address from Resend if you ask.
